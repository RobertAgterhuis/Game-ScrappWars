Knee-high on a planet of wrecks

You are SD-7, the maintenance scrapper of a relief freighter that fell out of the sky. Kessra is a sun-bleached desert under two suns, where two fleets fought for thirty years, and the wrecks they left are the size of hills.

Every part you salvage changes how you move, not just a number: magnet legs walk up a hull, a thruster carries you across a gap, spider legs climb what nothing else can. Your body is your skill tree, and the desert is the map of what it can do.

  • Parts, not statsThirteen parts in six sockets, swapped at a workbench or with a field kit in the desert.
  • A living desertDay and night, noon heat, sandstorms, eclipses, and something armoured that ploughs through the deep sand.
  • Story in the scrapTwenty-five memory logs, most of them read aloud by the crews who left them.
  • Your paceEasy, Medium, Hard or Ultra, switchable at any time. Keyboard and mouse or a controller.

SD-7

A small, rusty maintenance scrapper with one big round lens that focuses in little motor steps. It beeps how it feels: happy at a find, scared in a storm, proud after a repair. Wear shows on its body, and a repair cleans it up again.

WREN

The Patient Wren's ship mind, scattered across Kessra but still on the radio. She gives you every objective in her own voice, her signal starts weak and full of static, and she grows warmer as the story goes on.

The people you meet

The robed mechanics of Dunewell, whose talk changes as the story moves on; scrappers lost in storms that you dig out and that help you once a day; traders with contracts; and Mote, a dented little drone you can have built.

The journey

WREN talks you across Kessra on the radio. The main story is one road in seven stops and twenty-one objectives; the desert between them is yours to wander.

  1. The Scrap Plain

    Wake up beside the escape pod, scan for parts and climb the container field. Walk up the hull of the Patient Wren and bring out her signal core.

    Grapple arm, jump thruster, magnet legs, cutting torch.

  2. The Glass Plain

    Where the bombardment turned the sand to glass. An escape pod and an old observatory, and the sun thrown back at you from every side.

    Deflector shield, the focus lens.

  3. Canyon City

    A mining town in a gorge, its lifts dead, scrap hunters nesting on the terraces. The foundry at the top made power cells for the whole region.

    EMP pulse, a power cell.

  4. The caves

    Below the canyon, pitch black, lit only by the crystals that grow there and your own headlamp.

    A resonance crystal.

  5. The Salt Graveyard

    Battleships of both fleets in a dry salt sea, and storms that strip paint. A Sable cruiser on its side can be searched from the inside.

    The antenna coupler.

  6. The Crater Sea

    Soft sand and something that lives under it. Do not run.

    Spider legs.

  7. The Dawnbreaker

    The Directorate flagship, fallen across the plain. Throw the deck relays to wake the bridge, then climb the collapsed decks while the ship settles around you.

    The transmitter.

Off the road

Dunewell's market and houses, the green crater of Hollowmere and what lives in its swamp, the Scorch, two sealed supply vaults, five broken signal beacons, scrappers lost in storms, a scrap train that never stops, traders' contracts and photo jobs: thirty-two side jobs that pay in scrap, and places no objective sends you.

Parts are your skill tree

SD-7 has six sockets. What you fit decides where you can go, and every part wears out as you use it.

Legs
  • Wheels Fast on open sand, useless on walls.
  • Mag-Legs Walk up any metal surface; jump to let go.
  • Spider Legs Climb steep rock and sand, keep your footing in storms, tread quietly.
Back
  • Jump Thruster Jump again in mid-air, or hold to hover.
Arm
  • Grapple Arm Hook on and reel yourself in.
  • Cutting Torch Cut through glowing plates. Runs hot.
Head
  • Scanner Reveal salvage through walls, and chart the map around you.
Core
  • Solar Panel Recharge in direct sunlight.
  • Battery Cell A spare power cell strapped to SD-7's back for more energy.
  • Cooling Block Halves the sun's heat and cools faster in shade.
Defence
  • EMP Pulse Stun everything close by; a stunned hunter can be salvaged.
  • Deflector Shield Block bites and hits; attackers bounce off, dazed.
  • Blaster Fire an amber bolt where you look.

Energy brings you home

Everything you do costs charge. Solar panels, charge pads and energy caches keep you going; run flat and the rescue network brings you back to an explored base.

The sun is not your friend

Noon heat locks your systems until you cool down. Find shade, fit a cooling block, or travel at dusk.

Parts wear out

Broken legs make you limp. Repair at a workbench or a tinker's camp, for scrap, or pack a field kit to swap a part out in the desert.

The desert bites back

Scavengers shy from your headlamp and circle round in the dark, scrap hunters hunt in pairs, sandstorms push you around, and the deep worm hears every fast wheel.

Scrap is money

Salvage it and spend it on upgrades, repairs, a paint job, a helper drone, and an escape pod that slowly turns into a home.

A camera for the view

Photo mode with film looks, depth of field and a letterbox. Some jobs even ask for a picture.

Scrap Marks

Since version 0.10, SD-7 collects stamped steel tags for what it does on Kessra. They are earned in play only, the moment something happens, and all three save slots add to the same service record.

  • 60 marks in eight sets: Story, Exploration, Salvage and rescue, Survival, Craft, Trade, Photography and a few hidden ones.
  • 8 medals on canvas ribbons, one for every complete set, and a service rank from Class D up to "Last Scrapper".
  • Rewards for show only: two paint jobs, a rank stencil on SD-7's back plate, a photo look and frame, a planter for Mote, and a service board on the trophy wall at home. SD-7 plays exactly the same.

Notices wait for storms, cinematics and WREN, and can be made silent or switched off.

The story

For thirty years the Helion Directorate and the Sable Free Fleet fought over Kessra's sky. Squadrons burned above the dunes; on the sand, walkers and tanks fought for every ridge until the desert became a graveyard of machines.

Then came the Patient Wren: a neutral freighter carrying water, medicine and seed for the settlements. Each fleet was told a different lie about her. Both opened fire. She fell into the desert, and her crew was lost.

All but one small scrapper, still waiting for orders.

SD-7 wakes up with no ship to maintain. WREN, what is left of the freighter's mind, still reaches it on the radio, and she has a plan: with the Wren's signal core and a big enough transmitter, both fleets could be told the truth. The only transmitter left is on the Dawnbreaker, and its amplifier is burnt out. Four parts, scattered across the desert, could fix it.

On the way, the wrecks talk. Memory cores in pods, cabins and bridges hold the last words of pilots, miners, captains, a botanist and an admiral, and slowly they tell how the war really went, and how close it came to ending.

How it ends (spoilers)

The cease-fire was never the problem. The fleet's admiral had it drafted on the Dawnbreaker's bridge, agreed by both sides after the Wren went down, to go out at dawn on every channel. Then the last Sable walker hit the bridge, the amplifier burnt out, and the message never left.

And the lie had a sender. A signals officer who never reached the escape pod in the Crater Sea left the answer in the sand: she sent each fleet a different lie on the same night, on orders from her own side, because a relief ship that landed would have given the settlements water without asking whose side they were on. On the bridge, the admiral's last log admits he never asked who wrote it.

With the focus lens, the power cell, the resonance crystal and the antenna coupler, SD-7 rebuilds the amplifier. It throws the deck relays to bring the bridge back to life, and climbs the collapsed decks on spider legs while the ship settles and groans around it. Then it puts the Wren's signal core into the transmitter.

The broadcast needs all of WREN. She says goodbye before she goes out on every channel.

SIGNAL SENT

The camera pulls away into the sky. At sunset, beacons light up one after another across the desert, at every camp, station, scrapper and memory core, in a wave rolling out from the flagship. The Dawnbreaker's transmitter wakes one last time, and on every channel, in every fleet, WREN's own voice says it: no weapons aboard, cease-fire. It's over.

Three days later the first relief ship lands on Kessra. Its crew finds a small, rusty scrapper waiting by the escape pod, still maintaining.

When the game is over, and after

The main story is finished when the cease-fire is sent: the last of WREN's twenty-one objectives. The ending screen shows how many days you spent on Kessra, how many memories you recovered and how many scrap hunters you salvaged. Then the game simply goes on, with the radio quiet now, and a checklist in the journal shows every memory core, vault, beacon and corner of the map you have not found yet.

Help build ScrappWars

ScrappWars is a one-person project, shaped by the people who play it. You do not need to code to help: playing, reporting and saying what you think is the work that matters most right now.

Play it and tell how it went

Play the first hour without help and notice where you get stuck, what you do not understand and what you enjoy. Then write it down while it is fresh: your graphics card, the resolution and tier you played on, what you changed in the settings and why.

The game can keep a local session log (Settings, "Keep a local session log (never sent)"): how long each objective took, how often SD-7 ran flat and why, which parts broke, and where scrap came from and went. It is anonymous and stays on your PC. Attach it to a report if you like.

Report a bug from inside the game

Press Ctrl+Alt+B wherever it happens: in the menu, while playing or in photo mode. The game saves a screenshot, pauses and asks what went wrong. Open on GitHub opens a new bug report in your browser, already filled in with your text, the version, where SD-7 is and your settings, and opens the folder with the screenshot so you can drag it in.

Two boxes, both off unless you tick them, put your latest session log and a copy of your save next to the screenshot, so the bug can be replayed exactly. Nothing leaves your computer until you press Submit on GitHub.

Install and play

Install

  1. Download ScrappWars-0.11.0.0-windows-x64.zip from the newest release.
  2. Unpack it anywhere.
  3. Run ScrappWarsUpdater.exe in the unpacked folder. It checks every file, installs the game into %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\ScrappWars, adds it to the Start menu and starts it. No administrator rights needed; the unpacked folder can go afterwards.

Windows may show a SmartScreen warning, because the game is not code-signed: choose "More info" and then "Run anyway".

Updates

The game looks for a new version when the main menu opens; Settings, Updates turns that off, adds beta versions or checks by hand. Installing is always your choice. The download is checked against its signature and checksum, the old version is kept as a backup, and it comes back if anything goes wrong.

Your saves

Three save slots. Saves, settings, photos, bug report screenshots and session logs are kept in %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\ScrappWars by default. To keep them somewhere else, such as another drive, open Settings, Data folder: the game copies everything there, checks every file, and only then switches. Updates and uninstalling never touch your data folder.

Uninstall

Run ScrappWarsUpdater.exe --uninstall from the install folder. Your data folder stays; delete it by hand if you want it gone.

Controls

Play with keyboard and mouse or a game controller. Settings, Controls lets you pick Automatic (prompts follow whatever you touched last), Keyboard and mouse, or Game controller, and rebind almost every action to another key, mouse button, controller button, trigger or stick direction. If the input is taken, the game offers to swap the two. A live diagram shows what every key and button does and lights up as you press it, and every prompt in the game names the key or button you actually use.

  • Move
  • Jump and hover
  • Arm: grapple or torch
  • Scan
  • Interact
  • Headlamp
  • Throw scrap
  • Defence: EMP or shield
  • Repeat objective
  • Map and journal
  • Photo mode
  • Controls help

Press-instead-of-hold for hover, torch, shield and grapple, interface scale, subtitle size and background, screen shake, field of view, look speed and inversion for mouse and stick, and high-contrast objective markers are all in the settings.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. ScrappWars is free to download, play and pass on unchanged. There are no purchases in the game and no account is needed to play.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. The game plays fully offline. It only goes online to check for a new version when the main menu opens, and you can switch that off in Settings, Updates.

Does it run on Mac, Linux or Steam Deck?

There is only a Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit) version so far. If you would like another platform, say so in the ideas.

Why does Windows warn me before it starts?

The game is not code-signed, so SmartScreen does not know it yet. Choose "More info" and "Run anyway". The updater itself checks that every file is the one that was released: each release is signed, and a changed file is refused.

Will an update break my saves?

No. Updates never touch your data folder, and saves from older versions keep loading. The updater keeps the previous version as a backup and restores it if an update fails.

Can I play with a controller?

Yes. Prompts use neutral button names (A, B, X and Y by position). You can rebind it, and switch between controller and keyboard at any time.

How hard is it?

You choose Easy, Medium, Hard or Ultra at a new game and can switch at any time in the pause settings. Difficulty changes energy use, heat, wear, enemies and prices, and where SD-7 is brought back when it runs flat. The story, the map and the puzzles are the same on every difficulty.

Can I stream it or make videos?

Yes, including on channels that earn money from ads or donations. You can also share screenshots and photos you took in photo mode. The licence has the details.

Is the source code available?

No. The public repository holds the releases, the update channels, the release notes and this site; the source stays private. You can still shape the game through bug reports, discussions and playtests.

Is it a beta?

ScrappWars is complete from start to ending, and still growing. Stable versions are the default; Settings, Updates can add beta versions if you want to try new things first.
