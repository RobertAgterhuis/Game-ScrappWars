Knee-high on a planet of wrecks

You are SD-7, the maintenance scrapper of a relief freighter that fell out of the sky. Kessra is a sun-bleached desert where two fleets fought for thirty years, and the wrecks they left are the size of hills.

Every part you salvage changes how you move, not just a number: magnet legs walk up a hull, a thruster carries you across a gap, spider legs climb what nothing else can. Your body is your skill tree, and the desert is the map of what it can do.

  • Parts, not statsWheels, mag-legs, spider legs, thruster, grapple, torch, scanner, shield, EMP and more, swapped at a workbench.
  • A living desertDay and night, heat, sandstorms, eclipses, and something armoured that ploughs through the deep sand.
  • Story in the scrapTwenty memory cores, read aloud by the crews who left them.
  • Your paceEasy, Medium, Hard or Ultra, switchable at any time.

The journey

WREN, the freighter's scattered ship mind, talks you across Kessra on the radio. The main story is one road in seven stops; the desert between them is yours to wander.

  1. The Scrap Plain

    Wake up beside the escape pod, scan for parts and climb the container field. Walk up the hull of the Patient Wren and bring out her signal core.

    Grapple arm, jump thruster, magnet legs, cutting torch.

  2. The Glass Plain

    Where the bombardment turned the sand to glass. An escape pod and an old observatory, and the sun thrown back at you from every side.

    Deflector shield, the focus lens.

  3. Canyon City

    A mining town in a gorge, its lifts dead, scrap hunters nesting on the terraces. The foundry at the top made power cells for the whole region.

    EMP pulse, a power cell.

  4. The caves

    Below the canyon, pitch black, lit only by the crystals that grow there and your own headlamp.

    A resonance crystal.

  5. The Salt Graveyard

    Battleships of both fleets in a dry salt sea, and storms that strip paint. A Sable cruiser on its side can be searched from the inside.

    The antenna coupler.

  6. The Crater Sea

    Soft sand and something that lives under it. Do not run.

    Spider legs.

  7. The Dawnbreaker

    The Directorate flagship, fallen across the plain. Wake its rescue beacon, cut through the bulkhead and climb the collapsed decks to the bridge.

    The transmitter.

Off the road

Dunewell's market and houses, the green crater of Hollowmere and what lives in its swamp, the Scorch, sealed supply vaults, broken signal beacons, scrappers lost in storms, a scrap train that never stops, traders' contracts and photo jobs: side jobs that pay in scrap, and places no objective sends you.

How it plays

Energy brings you home

Everything you do costs charge. Solar panels, charge pads and energy caches keep you going; run flat and the rescue network brings you back to an explored base.

The sun is not your friend

Noon heat locks your systems until you cool down. Find shade, fit a cooling block, or travel at dusk.

Parts wear out

Every part takes wear. Broken legs make you limp; repair at a workbench or a tinker's camp, for scrap.

The desert bites back

Scavengers swarm your lamp at night, scrap hunters patrol, sandstorms push you around, lightning hits the Glass Plain, and the deep worm hears every fast wheel.

Scrap is money

Salvage it, sell services for it, buy upgrades, a paint job, a helper drone, and turn the escape pod into a home.

A camera for the view

Photo mode with film looks, depth of field and a letterbox. Some jobs even ask for a picture.

The story

For thirty years the Helion Directorate and the Sable Free Fleet fought over Kessra's sky. Squadrons burned above the dunes; on the sand, walkers and tanks fought for every ridge until the desert became a graveyard of machines.

Then came the Patient Wren: a neutral freighter carrying water, medicine and seed for the settlements. Both fleets were told she carried weapons. Both fleets opened fire. She fell into the desert, and her crew was lost.

All but one small scrapper, still waiting for orders.

SD-7 wakes up with no ship to maintain. WREN, what is left of the freighter's mind, still reaches it on the radio, and she has a plan: with the Wren's signal core and a big enough transmitter, both fleets could be told the truth. The only transmitter left is on the Dawnbreaker, and its amplifier is burnt out. Four parts, scattered across the desert, could fix it.

On the way, the wrecks talk. Memory cores in pods, cabins and bridges hold the last words of pilots, miners, captains, a botanist and an admiral, and slowly they tell how the war really went, and how close it came to ending.

How it ends (spoilers)

The cease-fire was never the problem. Admiral Veyl had it drafted on the Dawnbreaker's bridge, agreed by both sides after the Wren went down, to go out at dawn on every channel. Then the last Sable walker hit the bridge, the amplifier burnt out, and the message never left.

And the lie had a sender. A signals officer who never reached the escape pod in the Crater Sea left the answer in the sand: she told both fleets the same lie on the same night, on orders from her own side, because a relief ship that landed would have given the settlements water without asking whose side they were on. On the bridge, the admiral's last log admits he never asked who wrote it.

With the focus lens, the power cell, the resonance crystal and the antenna coupler, SD-7 rebuilds the amplifier. It throws the deck relays to bring the bridge back to life, and climbs the collapsed decks on spider legs while the ship settles and groans around it. Then it puts the Wren's signal core into the transmitter.

The broadcast needs all of WREN. She says goodbye before she goes out on every channel.

SIGNAL SENT

The camera pulls away into the sky. At sunset, beacons light up one after another across the desert, at every camp, station, scrapper and memory core, in a wave rolling out from the flagship. The Dawnbreaker's transmitter wakes one last time, and on every channel, in every fleet, WREN's own voice says it: no weapons aboard, cease-fire. It's over.

Three days later the first relief ship lands on Kessra. Its crew finds a small, rusty scrapper waiting by the escape pod, still maintaining.

When the game is over, and after

The main story is finished when the cease-fire is sent: the last of WREN's twenty-one objectives. The ending screen shows how many days you spent on Kessra, how many memories you recovered and how many scrap hunters you salvaged. Then the game simply goes on, with the radio quiet now: the message keeps going out, and every side job, memory core, vault and corner of the map you have not found is still there.

Install

  1. Download ScrappWars-0.10.0.0-windows-x64.zip from the newest release.
  2. Unpack it anywhere.
  3. Run ScrappWarsUpdater.exe in the unpacked folder. It checks every file, installs the game into %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\ScrappWars, adds it to the Start menu and starts it. No administrator rights needed.

Windows may show a SmartScreen warning, because the game is not code-signed: choose "More info" and then "Run anyway".

Updates

The game looks for a new version when the main menu opens; Settings, Updates turns that off, adds beta versions or checks by hand. Installing is always your choice, the download is checked against its signature, and the old version comes back if anything goes wrong.

Your saves

Saves, settings and photos are kept in %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\ScrappWars by default. To keep them somewhere else, such as another drive, open Settings, Data folder: the game copies everything there, checks the copy, and only then switches. Updates and uninstalling never touch your data folder.

Uninstall

Run ScrappWarsUpdater.exe --uninstall from the install folder.

Found a bug?

Report it from inside the game, right where it happens. Since version 0.5.0 fix 1, Ctrl+Alt+B works everywhere: in the menu, while playing and in photo mode.

  1. Press Ctrl+Alt+B. The game saves a screenshot of that moment and pauses.
  2. Give the bug a short title, say what happened and, if you like, what you expected.
  3. Press Open on GitHub. Your browser opens a new bug report, already filled in, and the folder with the screenshot opens beside it.
  4. Drag the screenshot into the report and press Submit new issue. This needs a free GitHub account.

What is sent

Only what the form shows: your text, the game's version, Windows version, graphics card and quality setting, where SD-7 is, the difficulty, the objective and the time of day. The last lines of the game log are optional and lose any folder paths and your Windows user name. Two more boxes, both off unless you tick them, put your latest session log and a copy of your save next to the screenshot, so a bug can be replayed exactly; you decide whether to drag them in. Nothing leaves your computer until you press Submit on GitHub; the game never posts anything by itself.

Without the game

Open a bug report on GitHub and fill in the same questions. Look through the open issues first: if your bug is already there, a comment or a thumbs-up helps just as much.

Check your version

On an older version? Update first (Settings, Updates): the bug may already be fixed. The release notes list every fix.
