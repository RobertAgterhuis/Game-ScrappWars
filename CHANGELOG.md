# Release notes

Every released version of ScrappWars, newest first. Downloads: [releases](https://github.com/RobertAgterhuis/Game-ScrappWars/releases).

## 0.9.0 (0.9.0.0, 2026-10-30)

The "clear the backlog" update: you choose how you play and where your game is kept, SD-7 walks and looks better than ever, and the big wrecks, the light and the frame rate all got a pass. Everything plays as before.

### Your game, your way

- **Choose your controller:** Settings > Controls now lets you pick Automatic, Keyboard & mouse or Game controller. Automatic follows whatever you touched last; an explicit choice plays with that device only, and the other one can never lock you out.
- **Rebind almost every action:** any action can be moved to another key, mouse button, controller button, trigger or stick direction; only the pause menu and the right-stick camera stay where they are. If the input is already taken, the game offers to swap the two. Each device can be reset on its own.
- **A live diagram** of the keyboard and mouse or the controller shows what every key and button does, and lights up as you press it; its labels never cross each other.
- **Every prompt follows your device:** hints, the map, photo mode and WREN's subtitles name the key or button you actually use.
- **Choose where your game is kept:** Settings > Data folder moves your saves, settings, photos and reports to a folder of your own. Every file is checked after the copy, nothing is ever overwritten, and if that folder goes missing (an unplugged drive), the game says so, saves in the default folder for that session and goes back to your folder once it is there again.
- **A fitting first graphics setting:** on its first start the game looks at your graphics card and picks a tier that runs well. A setting you chose yourself is never changed.

### SD-7

- **Real knees:** the spider legs fold at the knee and keep their feet planted on steps and slopes, also when limping.
- **The iris works:** SD-7's big lens opens in the dark and stops down in bright sunlight, step by step.
- **Paint you can see:** every paint job now reads on SD-7's body through the dust and the wear, not only on its head and trim.
- **The intro shows SD-7:** after the crash, a small scrapper wakes in an escape pod in the binary sunset, rolls out, focuses its lens and lights its lamp. The crash dust glows against the low sun, and the trailer now ends on SD-7.
- A new start-up screen with SD-7 on a sunset horizon.

### Kessra

- **The Patient Wren** is split wide open: a jagged rift with torn plating, a broken roof line and wreckage spilled toward the way in.
- **The Dawnbreaker** carries a fire-control house with a lattice mast, a conning house at the bow and armoured galleries with gun tubs along its flanks.
- **Light shafts** fall through the Rib Field's ribs, the Wren's hold, the flagship's frames, the salt-bound skeletons and the canyon gorge.
- **Canyon City's** homes are set back into the cliff instead of standing out as shelves from above.
- The Rib Field's reveal now starts once the rock no longer hides the ribs.
- **Creatures:** the sand worm has a bigger head that sheds sand as it rises, a boiling dust ring and a cheaper far view; the scrap hunter's head reads better from a distance.

### Light and look

- On the highest setting the big wrecks' second shadow softens with distance; SD-7's double shadow stays crisp.
- The moon fades out beside the sun instead of hanging there by day.
- The title screen's containers show their markings again.
- Warmer shade tarps at dusk, heat-tinted burns on dark paint, damp fresh mud on Dunewell's houses, and the stand-in transmitter screen is a proper tube.

### Performance

- Far fewer shadow draws everywhere: Dunewell at dusk drops from about 7,800 to 2,300 draw calls on the highest setting, and home at sunset from about 4,600 shadow draws to about 500.
- Hollowmere's jungle is cheaper on the highest setting.

### Fixes

- With the game controller chosen, the mouse no longer turns the camera.
- The breeze's sand streaks no longer turn into bright specks under a low sun.

## 0.8.0 (0.8.0.0, 2026-10-30)

The "Twin Sunset" update: every machine, wreck, creature and corner of Kessra has been rebuilt to look harder used, patched together and lived in, and the desert now ends every day with two suns going down together. Everything plays exactly as before.

### Two suns

- **Binary sunsets:** Kessra's small, pale-blue second sun now travels beside the big one, a little lower, and sets with it. At dawn and dusk SD-7 throws two overlapping shadows (on High and Ultra; on Ultra the big wrecks and rocks do too).
- **Vashka** hangs larger in the sky in soft cream and orange bands, and glows faintly at night.
- **Salt and heat:** salt pans crust the low ground of the open desert, the far horizon boils with heat shimmer and mirage, and a sandstorm tears the clouds apart into an ochre haze.

### SD-7 and its parts

- **A new face:** SD-7 looks out through one big round camera lens that focuses in little motor steps, hunts when you scan and goes still when it runs flat.
- **A jury-rigged body:** dented plates, hand-laid welds, bare battery leads, a rusty dish on its back. When a part is close to breaking, SD-7 looks it: a cover plate hangs off its last bolt, the lens cracks, the worst part smokes and the leads spark. A repair cleans it up again.
- **Parts that work:** every upgrade is a worn machine you can watch at work. Magnet pads spark, spider legs hiss, the thruster coughs dirty flame, the torch tip glows and spits, the grapple's claw flies out on a cable and winches back, the scanner's lenses click, blaster bolts fly lit across the sand and the shield ripples amber where it is hit.
- **Feet on the ground:** SD-7 tilts with the slope, its wheels roll true and its feet plant, a broken leg limps visibly, and it leaves tracks in the sand that the wind fills in.
- **Wear that stays put:** chips, rust and burns now stay on moving machines instead of sliding over them.

### The people and machines of Kessra

- **WREN's radio** squelches open and shut, crackles and distorts on a weak signal, and her panel is a green phosphor scope.
- **Mote** is a dented little box with one amber eye on a sputtering lift field.
- **Dunewell's mechanics** wear heavy robes, masks, goggles and greasy tool belts. The stranded scrappers lie buried to their shoulders in the sand.
- **Scavengers** click their pincers and rear back hissing from your headlamp; **scrap hunters** are lean, dog-like hunters whose twin sensors follow you; the sand worm ploughs with a heavy, plated wedge of a snout.
- **Rival fleets:** the Directorate's wrecks are hard-edged slate and navy with brass trim and bare bellies; the Sable fleet's are salvage, mismatched plates bolted on and canvas strapped down for roofs.

### Places

- **Home:** the escape pod's hatch is now a ramp, and you can drive inside its cramped cabin with hammocks, terminals and a workbench full of loose tools.
- **Dunewell** has grown into a dense town of mud domes, every house with a turning wind-catcher, and the stalls hang with coarse burlap.
- **Canyon City** climbs its deep orange walls: homes and rooms carved into the rock, clanking chain lifts and an iron foundry smoking on the summit.
- **The Dawnbreaker** has a collapsed corridor in its torn flank, wiring dangling in the wind and sand streaming through; the **Patient Wren** lies broken in half with her machinery spilled into the dunes; the **Seedbearer** breathes thick white mist over Hollowmere's vine-choked mangroves.
- **The Glass Plain's** obsidian spires arc lightning across the plain in a storm; tall salt pillars weave through ship skeletons in the **Salt Graveyard**; the **Crater Sea** swirls down into a sinking funnel; **the Scorch** is littered with fallen walkers and melted beam holes.
- **The Underground Caves and the Long Night** are truly dark now: crystals, red alarm lights, flickering terminals and your headlamp are all you see by.
- **Memory cores** are heavy, scratched metal cylinders with one button and a small speaker. The open desert has jagged arches, the bleached bones of old grazers and sand trailing off the dune crests.

### Fixes

- The shield's flash no longer stays lit after the shield drops, and the salt pans no longer whiten settlements and roads.

## 0.7.0 (0.7.0.0, 2026-10-29)

The "Used Universe" update: Kessra and everything on it looks older, harder used and more lived in. Everything plays exactly as before.

### A world that has been used

- **Wear that tells a story:** paint chips off along edges down to primer, bare steel and rust; rust runs down below chips and rivets; matte black burn streaks mark hulls and weapons; sand settles in every seam and joint, in the colour of the ground around it.
- **SD-7 shows what it went through:** a part that is wearing out looks it, and a repaired part is clean again.
- **SD-7's new body,** cobbled together from mismatched freighter plating: an old riveted hull plate with its stencil, a cargo plate, a bare replacement sheet, welds with heat-blued halos and bolted patches.
- **Coarse canvas:** every tarp, awning, tent and pennant is sun-bleached, stained, patched and torn at the edges.

### Machines with dials

- **Every screen is a CRT** with scan lines and phosphor glow, every readout an analog dial with a needle. Workbenches, beacons, charge pads, coolant stations, camps, the market, the vaults, the scrap train and the Dawnbreaker's bridge got cable bundles, toggles, push buttons and hydraulic rams.
- Some needles show the real thing: the charge going into SD-7, the coolant left in a station, the power on a relay.

### Landmarks

- **Dunewell's houses are mud,** trowelled and patched, and five of them now have mechanical wind-catcher towers whose cowls turn into the wind.
- **The Dawnbreaker** shows its heavy ribs where the hull is torn open, and its stern carries huge thruster bells with cooling pipes, rams and heat-blued, sooted throats.

### Light

- **A second, small sun:** Kessra's minor sun, pale and cool, crosses the sky a little behind the big one.
- Richer contrast at every hour, dust that catches the light, and glow only where a real lamp or screen shines.

### Fixes

- Settings, menus and photo mode looks all work with the new look; no more warnings in the log at Dunewell.

## 0.6.0 (0.6.0.0, 2026-10-29)

The first big update after release: a full director's pass over the story, the play, the look and the controls.

### Story

- **Who lied?** A new memory core in the Crater Sea answers the question both fleets asked, and the admiral's last log now half admits it.
- **WREN's farewell.** The broadcast needs all of her: she says goodbye when her core goes into the transmitter, and in the epilogue her own voice sends the cease-fire.
- **SD-7 speaks for itself** in four new maintenance logs, and from mid-game WREN picks up transmissions from the fleets still fighting overhead.
- Each amplifier part now comes with its own story, and later directions are vaguer: the scanner finds the last step.
- **Dunewell talks.** Five townsfolk have something to say, and it changes as the story moves on.
- The epilogue ends with a line about what you did on Kessra.
- Kessra's machines are now called **scrappers**.

### Play

- **A new final act:** throw the deck relays to power the Dawnbreaker's bridge, then climb while the ship settles, debris falls (watch the marks), a storm rises and the worm circles the hull.
- **Enemies with tactics:** scavengers shy from your headlamp beam and circle round to strike from the dark; scrap hunters hunt in pairs, one holding while the other flanks.
- **Field kits:** pack one at a workbench and swap a part once out in the desert.
- **After the end:** a checklist in the journal and on the save slot shows what is still out there.
- Fairer economy (freight once per day, storm scrap near you), zones that are always right after a rescue or a load, and hunters that patrol their own regions again.

### Look

- A modelled, wrecked bridge on the Dawnbreaker that comes to life when you power it.
- Dunewell rebuilt from the wrecks: wind-catcher towers, stepped and vaulted houses, hull-plate walls.
- Storms you can see through near by, and SD-7 lit by its own lamp in the dust.
- Leaves and cloth fade out between the camera and SD-7 instead of filling the screen.
- Reveal shots that frame the wreck, a richer noon, a cooler dusk, natural canyon strata, salvage ramps, and lookouts where the camera shows the view when you stop.
- Lower video memory and fewer draw calls with the same image.

### Controls and comfort

- The interface scales with the window, plus a UI scale setting.
- Subtitle size and background, press-instead-of-hold for hover, torch, shield and grapple, screen shake on/off, field of view, high-contrast markers, objective markers on/after a while/off.
- Display page: render scale (FSR), vsync, fps limit, volumetric clouds and GI switches.
- Separate gamepad stick speed, inversion and deadzone.
- A journal next to the map, clearer warnings, and the bug reporter (Ctrl+Alt+B) can attach your save and session log.

## 0.5.0 fix 1 (0.5.0.1, 2026-10-29)

### New

- **Report a bug from inside the game.** Press Ctrl+Alt+B wherever something
  goes wrong. The game saves a screenshot, pauses and asks what happened. "Open
  on GitHub" opens a new issue in your browser, already filled in with your text
  and the game's version, place and settings (optionally the last lines of the
  log, without paths or your user name), and shows the screenshot in its folder
  so you can drag it in. You send the issue with your own GitHub account; the
  game never posts anything by itself.
- The shortcut is listed on the controls page and in the controls help.
